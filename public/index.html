What is actually on it

From across the room it's a texture. Up close it's every word you said.

The body of the poster is the conversation, one message per line, in each of your two colours — printed at three point, which is small enough to fit and just big enough to read with your nose against the paper. Underneath sit four things you actually said, found by counting: the first message, the message that ended the longest silence, the latest either of you was ever awake, and the phrase one of you can't stop using.

This example is built from an invented chat, so nobody's real messages are on display.

Getting the file

Exporting a chat takes about twenty seconds.

WhatsApp has this built in. Choose Without media — the poster is made from the words, and the file stays small.

On iPhone

  1. Open the chat, then tap the contact's name at the top.
  2. Scroll down and tap Export Chat.
  3. Choose Without Media.
  4. Tap Save to Files, then drop that .zip here.

On Android

  1. Open the chat and tap the ⋮ menu, top right.
  2. Tap More, then Export chat.
  3. Choose Without media.
  4. Save it, then drop that .txt here.

Questions

The ones worth answering.

Is my conversation really private?

Yes, and not as a promise — as a fact about how the page is built. Longthread is one static page. The reading, the counting and the drawing all happen in JavaScript inside your own browser. There is no server that receives chats, because there is no server at all. You can confirm it: open your browser's network panel before you drop the file and watch that nothing is sent.

What about group chats?

They work, but the poster is built for two. It picks whoever wrote the most messages and whoever wrote the second most, draws those two, and tells you it has done so. A poster of eleven people is a different design, and this isn't it.

Does it handle other languages and emoji?

Yes. Message counts, timings, dates and emoji are correct in any language and any script. One caveat worth knowing before you buy: the "most-used words" line filters out common filler words in English and romanised Hindi only, so in other languages that line will show ordinary words like "the" and "and" rather than anything characteristic. Everything else on the poster is unaffected.

Which exports does it read?

WhatsApp exports from iPhone and Android, in any date format the app produces — day-first, month-first, 12-hour, 24-hour, dotted or slashed. It works out which one your file uses. It does not currently read exports from Telegram, iMessage or Signal.

How big a chat can it handle?

Tested to 250,000 messages, which parse in about two seconds. Very large exports on an older phone may run out of memory — if that happens, try it on a computer.

Does it really print every single message?

If they fit, yes — and the caption on the poster says so. About 600 lines fit at a size that stays readable on paper, so a chat shorter than that is printed in full. Above it, the poster takes an even spread across the whole span and tells you exactly what it did: “600 of the 40,000 things you said — one in every 67”. Printing 40,000 messages on A4 would mean type nobody could read, and claiming otherwise would be a lie you'd notice the moment it arrived.
